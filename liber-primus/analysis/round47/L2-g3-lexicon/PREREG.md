## 1. The fragment convention (pages that open mid-word)

Pages 1, 2, 4, 5 (of the G3 range 0–5) open mid-word: the first printed word starts on the previous page
(fragments on the page: 5 of 6, 2 of 5, 2 of 5, 2 of 3 runes). Pages 0 and 3 open on a word boundary. Every row here
restarts its key schedule per page (word index k = 0 at the page's first word; G3 also resets `prev = 0`, `D = 0`).
So "which word is word 0 of page p" decides the decode. Three conventions, all run, none privileged:

| id | name | page p's unit starts at | word 0 is | later page breaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C0** | split (legacy) | page start | the on-page fragment, as its own word | split again at every `%` (mapcommon rule) |
| **CS** | word belongs to the page it **starts** on (L1/R41 carry-over) | first word that starts on page p | that word | words joined (`words.py`) |
| **CE** | word belongs to the page it **ends** on | the wrapped word's first rune (on page p−1) | the whole wrapped word | words joined (`words.py`) |

Justification. C0 is exactly what R38/R39 ran (mapcommon splits at `/%`), so its real decodes are the recorded ones; it
is the coherent reading "the encipherer restarted the key at the physical page and treated the fragment as a word".
CS is the convention L1 used for R41 (a word lives on the page it starts on; the fragment's runes belong to page p−1's
schedule and are not part of page p's unit). CE is its mirror (the key restart happens at the start of the word that
completes on page p). There is no evidence on the held artifact that picks one, so the negative must be stated per
convention. (L1's R37 convention "C1" — fragment as word 0 with its full printed length as a *length value* — differs
from C0 only for rho3 length values; G3 and the three lexicon rows use no length value, so C1 ≡ C0 here.)

Unit end (sets the L=240 gate length = min(240, unit length)): C0 = page end; CS = end of the last word that starts
on page p; CE = end of the last word that ends on page p. Key arrays are built over 1,100 runes from the unit start
(NKEY), as in the original lanes; beyond the unit end the lengths continue with the same convention.

## 2. Aiming Test

**Q1 — What would a hit look like, and would this instrument recognise it?**
A hit is a page-unit decode by the row's own decoder and key glue scoring above the bar. The recognition rule is each
lane's own `judge()` (identical in R37/R38/R39 code): pmax120 ≥ bar_v3(1e6,120,rel) AND pmax240 ≥ bar_v3(1e6,240,rel)
AND recovery240 ≥ 0.90 (direct modes judged against the keyskip1 bar). Plants are English (`data/pride.txt`, B6 EN
mapping, L1's `make_plant`) laid over LP2's real 12,956-rune layout, rendered in krisyotam format, parsed back by
`words.py`; the encipherer is the lane's own (`run_gaps.g3_enc_direct` / `g3_enc_a3`; `round38|39 enciphers.enc`) run on
the TRUE words; the decoder is the lane's own (`run_gaps.g3_run`; `sweep.dec_shift` / `dec_a3`) given the lengths parsed
from the print. No lengths are handed over directly.

## 3. Plant design (frozen)

- **Layout pages.** G3: page 0 (control: all conventions coincide) and the mid-word pages 1, 2, 4, 5. Lexicon rows:
  page 0 and mid-word pages 1, 2 (the rows only ever decoded pages 0–2).
- **Matched arm.** For each regime ∈ {a, b, b′}, each layout page, each convention ∈ {C0, CS, CE} (page 0: one
  convention), and rep ∈ {0, 1}: one fresh `make_plant` stream (seeded `Random(47200 + …)`); on it, every cell of the
  row is planted once with random s (A1/A2: 0..28; lexicon A3: 0..725; G3: s = 0 as in the row), random σ, and for
  A3 a random relation of the row's set (G3: ks1/ks2; lexicon: ks1). The encipherer builds the unit and its word list
  from the TRUE plant words under the convention; the decoder builds them from `words.py` on the rendered print under
  the same convention. Plaintext = the plant's runes from the unit start, 240 runes; ciphertext = encipher(...)[:240];
  decode C[:120] and C[:240]; `judge()`.
  Per cell per regime: G3 2 + 4×3×2 = 26 plants; lexicon 2 + 2×3×2 = 14 plants.
- **Mismatch arm (regime a only).** Encipher under convention X, decode under Y ≠ X, mid-word pages, 1 rep: shows
  whether the convention is load-bearing. Reported, not used for verdicts.
- **Power** = fraction recognised, Wilson 95 % reported, per row × regime × convention (mid-word pages) and page 0.

## 4. Pass/fail and verdict rules (fixed now)

- Per row × regime × convention: power ≥ 0.90 → the row's negative **holds** under that regime and convention (within
  its coverage); 0.50 ≤ power < 0.90 → **weakened**; < 0.50 → **conditional** (the negative does not bind there).
  A row's page-0 regime-(a) power < 0.90 makes that row's cells **uninformative**, not negative.
- **Real re-runs (CS, CE).** Screen L = 120. Primary bar = bar_v3 at the **combined family N** (original decodes + this
  lane's new decodes, per relation/group) at L = 120: G3 A3 per relation 96 + 128 = 224, G3 direct 384 + 512 = 896;
  R38 shift 2,088 + 2,784 = 4,872, R38 A3 26,136 + 34,848 = 60,984; R39 shift 1,044 + 1,392 = 2,436, R39 A3
  13,068 + 17,424 = 30,492. Also reported against the original-N bar. Any decode ≥ primary bar is re-run at the gate
  L = min(240, unit length) and compared with bar_v3(combined N, L_gate); a gate pass is **flagged-for-oracle**
  (`HUMAN-ORACLE.md`: bundle in `ORACLE-<id>/`, lane stops on that space), never certified.
- Every real decode is persisted as a SWEEPROW store (R3 statistics via `AD.to_row`), validated with
  `AD.validate_store` and `validate_ledger.check_r3_store`.
- No decode sweep beyond these rows' own scope. No edits outside this folder. No `LEDGER.json` edit. No commit.
